Field notes on reading in the margins

Reading on paper changes what I do with my hands. On a screen I highlight, and the highlight feels like work even when nothing stuck. On paper I write in the margins, and the margin is small enough that I have to decide what the paragraph actually said before I can respond to it.

The e-ink tablet sits between the two. It is paper in the ways that matter for attention: no notifications, no tabs, no infinite scroll. It is a screen in the ways that matter for storage: every note is searchable later, and nothing is lost in a drawer.

Three habits stuck after a month:

1. Read the conclusion first, then decide whether the argument deserves the whole evening.

2. Write one question per page, even when the page is good.

3. Close the session with a three-line summary at the end of the document, in my own words.

The summary is the habit that pays. When I return to an article weeks later, the three lines tell me whether to read it again or to move on. Most of the time they are enough.
